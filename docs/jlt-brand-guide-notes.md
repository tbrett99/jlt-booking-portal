# JLT Brand Guide Notes

Source reviewed: `/home/ubuntu/upload/JLT_Branding_Chosen.pdf`.

- The brand direction is **minimal, premium, and editorial**, with generous whitespace and calm composition rather than dense utility styling.
- The core palette appears to centre on **soft mint/aqua**, **warm cream/off-white**, **black/charcoal typography**, and occasional **blush-peach accents**.
- The visual system uses a **high-contrast serif headline style** paired with a **clean, restrained sans-serif supporting type**.
- The logo treatment is predominantly **black on light neutral backgrounds**, with the mint tone used as a feature surface rather than a constant full-page fill.
- Supporting compositions use **layered paper-like panels, soft circular colour fields, and editorial collage framing** rather than hard dashboard cards.
- The overall mood is **luxury travel, polished print editorial, and independent boutique agency**, not discount-led or overtly corporate.
- Any consumer-site refresh should therefore favour **large serif section headings, elegant spacing, muted premium surfaces, understated motion, and stronger photographic framing**.
- The campaign examples introduce bolder editorial colour moments: deep **ink/teal**, muted **cobalt**, warm **apricot**, saturated **aqua**, and controlled **acid-yellow** typography accents. These should be used sparingly as feature moments rather than as the primary reading surface.
- The wordmark and campaign pages use expressive **high-contrast serif display type** in oversized, cropped or rotated compositions, supported by compact uppercase sans-serif metadata.
- The brand guide also positions JLT as a confident umbrella for independent agencies and travel lifestyles. The public site should retain its consumer clarity but use stronger magazine-style labels, asymmetric visual rhythm, and intentional editorial framing.
